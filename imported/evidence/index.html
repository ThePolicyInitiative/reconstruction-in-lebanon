Infrastructure, schools, health and water

2006: 145 bridges and overpasses and around 600 km of roads were reported damaged or destroyed; 900 factories, markets, farms and other commercial buildings and 23 fuel stations were also reported. A combined category of 29 facilities includes airports, ports, water and sewage facilities, dams and electrical plants. OCHA update.

2024 national RDNA: 930 km of roads damaged (216 destroyed and 714 partly damaged), 6 bridges damaged, 59 education assets destroyed and 299 partly damaged, and 298 health assets destroyed and 587 partly damaged. Health assets include pharmacies and other facilities; these are not hospital-only counts. World Bank RDNA.

Beirut and Mount Lebanon, 2026: 146 destroyed buildings, 264 partially damaged buildings and 54 separately documented targeted apartments; 3,168 destroyed and 4,437 partially damaged housing units; 648,942 m³ of debris and US$365 million in estimated building damage. The imagery comparison ends 14 April. UNDP assessment.

Area affected: burned land is not destroyed urban area

No directly comparable nationwide “urban area destroyed” total in km² was verified for all three periods. Available land figures describe different things.

ScopeReported hectaresSquare kilometres
2006 forest affected by fire: alternative contemporary inventories1,800–2,338 ha18.00–23.38 km²
2023–2024 burned land: CNRS-L southern map8,556 ha85.56 km²
2024–2026 ceasefire interval: same map160 ha1.60 km²
Since 2 March 2026: map published 26 May1,914 ha19.14 km²

One hectare = 0.01 km². The 2006 measure concerns forest; later figures concern burned land. Coverage and land categories differ, so ratios are not measures of relative urban destruction. World Bank environmental assessment · CNRS-L fire map.

The 2006 oil spill affected about 150 km of shoreline: a linear distance, not 150 km² of land. UN reporting.

Repeated destruction, heritage and comparison limits

CNRS-L counted 353 completely destroyed buildings in Beirut’s southern suburbs during the 2023–2024 offensive, a local subset. UNESCO’s March 2025 mission documented destruction of Baalbek’s Manchieh heritage building and the entrance to Bustan el-Khan; this does not establish destruction of the entire Roman complex. CNRS-L · UNESCO.

Amnesty’s investigation covers 1 October 2024–26 January 2025 and reports over 10,000 heavily damaged or destroyed structures across 24 municipalities. It overlaps other inventories and crosses the ceasefire, so it is not an additional total to add. Amnesty investigation.

Bellingcat found heavy damage or flattening in 46 of 54 examined towns and villages within the reported Yellow Line in May 2026. This does not mean all 46 settlements were completely erased. Bellingcat investigation.

2024’s 45,400 destroyed housing units are roughly three times the early 2006 estimate of 15,000. Different methods and reporting dates prevent treating this ratio as exact. A consistent comparison still lacks a nationwide 2026 census through October, common destroyed-only building classes, nationwide destroyed urban-area polygons and property-level tracking of repeated damage and repairs.
